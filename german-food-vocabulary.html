German Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

Brot, Käse, Kaffee and die Rechnung: the German food words you need for bakeries, cafés and restaurants, each with its gender.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

German food vocabulary rewards a little pattern-spotting. Many words are built from smaller ones you already know: Apfel (apple) plus Saft (juice) gives Apfelsaft; Abend (evening) plus Essen (food, meal) gives Abendessen, dinner. Once you know thirty basic nouns, you can understand a surprising share of a German menu.

All German nouns are written with a capital letter, and each has one of three genders: der (masculine), die (feminine) or das (neuter). There is no reliable way to guess the gender of most food words, so learn the article as part of the word. In the hints, "kh" stands for the soft throat sound in ich or Milch, and "ü" is said like "ee" with rounded lips.

Usage notes

Compound nouns take the gender of the last part

In a German compound, the final word decides the gender: der Apfel + der Saft = der Apfelsaft; der Abend + das Essen = das Abendessen; die Speise + die Karte = die Speisekarte. Our article on German compound words explains the pattern in more detail.

Kein, not nicht, before nouns

To say you don't eat or drink something, German normally uses kein in front of the noun: Ich esse kein Fleisch, Ich trinke keinen Alkohol. Nicht is used with verbs, adjectives and nouns that already have a definite article: Ich esse das nicht.

Ordering politely: Ich hätte gern

Ich hätte gern… (literally "I would gladly have…") is the standard polite way to order. Notice the accusative after it: einen Kaffee (masculine), eine Suppe, ein Bier. Ich nehme… (I'll take) is also very common. When the food arrives, people wish each other Guten Appetit!

Paying: zusammen oder getrennt?

When you ask for the bill in a group, the server will often ask Zusammen oder getrennt? (Together or separately?). Splitting the bill item by item is completely normal in German-speaking countries. When you pay in cash, saying Stimmt so ("that's fine as it is") tells the server to keep the change as a tip.

Plurals worth knowing

German plurals are irregular enough to be worth learning with each noun. From this list: das Ei → die Eier, der Apfel → die Äpfel, die Kartoffel → die Kartoffeln, das Brot → die Brote (loaves) and das Bier → die Biere. Das Gemüse is normally used in the singular, even though English says "vegetables".
