    📖 About Vietnamese

    Why learn Vietnamese with Flashlingo?

    Vietnamese is the national language of Vietnam, a country of around 100 million people, and is also spoken by large overseas communities in the United States, Australia, France and elsewhere. It's one of the most widely spoken languages in Southeast Asia and increasingly useful for travel, business and family ties across the region.

    How the Vietnamese levels work

    The Vietnamese deck has three levels of 500 words each, so you can start where you actually are.

    • Beginner — 500 words across 24 topics: everyday essentials such as food, home, transport, numbers and greetings. For example: táo (apple), xe đạp (bicycle), điện thoại (phone).
    • Intermediate — 500 words across 19 topics: vocabulary for work, travel, technology, health, money and the news. For example: làm việc từ xa (remote work), hộ chiếu (passport), ứng dụng (app).
    • Expert — 500 words across 17 topics: formal and academic vocabulary for law, science, philosophy, economics and the arts. For example: án lệ (case law), ý chí tự do (free will), lỗ đen (black hole).

    Much of the formal vocabulary is Sino-Vietnamese — built from roots borrowed from Chinese centuries ago — so words such as sinh học (biology), hóa học (chemistry) and ngôn ngữ học (linguistics) all share the root học (study).

    Tips for learning Vietnamese vocabulary faster

    • Vietnamese is written in the Latin alphabet (chữ Quốc ngữ), with extra letters and accent marks — so you can start reading straight away, but the marks matter.
    • Tone marks change meaning entirely: ba (three) and bà (grandmother) differ only by tone, and vowel marks matter too — mua (to buy) versus mưa (rain).
    • Verbs never change form for person or tense, so once you know a word you can use it right away — time is shown with separate words such as hôm qua (yesterday) or ngày mai (tomorrow).
    • Many words are built by combining simpler ones: máy bay (plane) is literally "machine" + "fly", and bữa sáng (breakfast) is "meal" + "morning".
    • In Hard mode you can type without accents and still score — you'll just earn fewer points than for a perfectly accented answer.

    A few words to start with

    xin chào (hello), cảm ơn (thank you), vâng (yes), xin lỗi (excuse me / sorry) and tạm biệt (goodbye) are a good everyday starting set.

    FAQ

    Do I have to type the accents in Hard mode?

    No. An answer without accents is accepted as a near-miss and still earns points; a fully accented answer earns the maximum.

    Which dialect does the deck use?

    Words follow standard written Vietnamese, which is based mainly on the Hanoi (northern) variety. Southern speakers sometimes use different everyday words — for example lợn (pig) is heo in the south — but written Vietnamese is understood everywhere.

    Is Vietnamese hard for English speakers?

    Pronunciation and tones take practice, but grammar is friendly: no verb conjugation, no plural endings and no grammatical gender.
