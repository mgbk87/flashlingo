How We Build Our Word Lists

Where the words come from, how they are checked, and what to do if you spot a mistake.

What is in each deck

Every Flashlingo language has 1,500 words, split into three levels of 500:

LevelTopicsFocus
🌱 Beginner24Numbers, colours, family, food, home, greetings, common verbs and adjectives
📘 Intermediate19Work, travel, health, technology, money, media, emotions and relationships
🎓 Expert17Law, politics, science, philosophy, economics, the arts and academic vocabulary

All decks start from the same list of English concepts, so the levels are comparable across languages. The words are then adapted to each language, as described below, so the decks are not identical.

How words are checked

Our newer decks, Korean, Vietnamese, Indonesian and Hindi, are checked against Kaikki.org, which publishes the dictionary data from Wiktionary in a form that can be searched and compared in bulk. For each word we check that:

  • the word exists as a dictionary entry in that language;
  • at least one of its definitions matches the English shown on the card;
  • multi-word terms are made only of words that are themselves dictionary entries;
  • no word appears twice within the same level.

When a concept has no good single translation, we do not make one up. We either use the established term that the dictionary gives, or replace the concept with a different word from the same topic. That is why some cards differ slightly between languages.

Our first eight decks, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Mandarin, Japanese and Malay, were built before we adopted this process. We are reviewing them against the same dictionary data, language by language, starting in November 2026.

Romanization and spelling

  • Korean uses Revised Romanization, the official system in South Korea, which spells words as they sound.
  • Hindi uses the romanization from the Wiktionary entry, which marks long vowels (ā), retroflex sounds (ṭ, ḍ) and nasal vowels (ã).
  • Mandarin uses pinyin with tone marks, and Japanese uses romaji.
  • For French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and German, nouns are shown with their article, because the article tells you the gender.
  • Where a language has regional standards, a deck follows one: the Malay deck follows Malaysian usage, and the Portuguese deck follows European Portuguese.

Limits worth knowing

A flashcard shows one translation, but real words often have several meanings, and the best translation depends on context. Treat each card as a starting point: once you know a word, notice how it is used in real sentences. Dictionary data also changes over time as Wiktionary editors improve it, so we recheck the decks when we update them.

Found a mistake?

Accuracy matters more to us than size. If a word or translation looks wrong, please contact us with the language, level, category and word. Every report is checked and corrections go into the next update.
