Portuguese Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

Pão, queijo, arroz e feijão: everyday Portuguese food words, with the Brazilian and European versions clearly marked wherever they differ.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

Portuguese is the main language of Brazil and Portugal, and the two countries share most of their food vocabulary. Bread is o pão, cheese is o queijo and rice is o arroz on both sides of the Atlantic. Some very common words do differ, though, starting with the first meal of the day: o café da manhã in Brazil and o pequeno-almoço in Portugal. We mark these with (BR) and (PT).

Nouns are masculine or feminine and take o or a as their article (os/as in the plural). The sound hints are rough guides based on Brazilian pronunciation (words marked PT get a European hint); European Portuguese tends to reduce unstressed vowels much more, so a word like leite sounds closer to "LAYT" in Lisbon. The tilde (ã, õ) marks a nasal vowel.

Usage notes

Brazil vs Portugal: the key pairs

The differences you will meet most often at the table are: breakfast (café da manhã BR / pequeno-almoço PT), juice (suco BR / sumo PT), menu (cardápio BR / ementa PT, where menu is also used) and waiter (garçom BR / empregado de mesa PT). Pick the set for the country you plan to visit, but learn to recognise both.

Ordering coffee

In Brazil, a small black coffee is often called um cafezinho, and it is commonly offered to guests. In Lisbon, an espresso is traditionally uma bica, while elsewhere in Portugal people often simply say um café.

Where the little words go

Brazilians usually put the object pronoun before the verb (Pode me trazer…?), while in Portugal it typically follows the verb with a hyphen (Pode trazer-me…?). Both sentences above mean the same thing.

Plurals of -ão words

Most nouns just add -s in the plural: o ovo → os ovos, a maçã → as maçãs. Words ending in -ão are less predictable, and two of the most common food words take different endings: o pão → os pães (breads, loaves) and o feijão → os feijões (beans, though the singular feijão is usual on menus).

Queria for polite requests

Eu queria… (literally "I wanted") is the everyday polite way to ask for something in both countries, much like English "I'd like". In Brazil, Vou querer… (I'm going to want) is a very natural way to order a dish.
