Japanese Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

ご飯, お茶, ラーメン and お会計: the Japanese words and phrases you need to order food, written in kanji and kana with romaji.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

Japanese food vocabulary is a good place to see the three writing systems working together. Native and long-established words are usually written in kanji, such as 魚 (sakana, fish) or 野菜 (yasai, vegetables). Words borrowed from other languages are written in katakana, such as パン (pan, bread, originally from Portuguese) or コーヒー (kōhī, coffee). Our guide to Japanese writing systems explains how hiragana, katakana and kanji fit together.

Japanese nouns have no gender, no articles and usually no plural form, which makes food words simple to learn. What matters more is politeness. Some words commonly take the polite prefix お or ご, such as お茶 (ocha, tea) and ご飯 (gohan, rice or a meal). In the table, the reading in kana follows the kanji, and romaji uses a macron (ō, ū) to show long vowels.

Usage notes

いただきます and ごちそうさまでした

Before eating, people say いただきます (itadakimasu), a set phrase of thanks often compared to "let's eat". After the meal they say ごちそうさまでした (gochisōsama deshita), thanking whoever prepared or paid for the food. You can say it to restaurant staff as you leave, too.

Counting with ひとつ, ふたつ

When ordering, the easiest counters are the native Japanese numbers ending in つ: 一つ hitotsu (one), 二つ futatsu (two), 三つ mittsu (three). They work for most dishes and drinks: ビールを二つください (two beers, please).

ご飯 means rice and meal

ご飯 gohan is cooked rice, but it also means a meal in general. That is why breakfast, lunch and dinner are 朝ご飯, 昼ご飯 and 晩ご飯 (morning, noon and evening + meal). In very casual speech, more often from men, you may also hear 朝飯 asameshi.

お酒 is not always sake

お酒 osake can mean alcoholic drinks in general. When Japanese people mean the rice drink specifically, they often say 日本酒 nihonshu. If someone asks お酒は飲みますか, they are asking whether you drink alcohol.

Dictionary form and polite form

Dictionaries list verbs in their plain form, like 食べる taberu and 飲む nomu. In polite conversation you use the -masu form: 食べます tabemasu (I eat), 飲みます nomimasu (I drink). The negative is -masen: 食べません tabemasen (I don't eat), 飲みません nomimasen.
