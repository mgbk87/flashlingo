    📖 About French

    Why learn French with Flashlingo?

    French is spoken by roughly 320 million people across five continents and is an official language in 29 countries and territories, from France and Belgium to Senegal, Québec and Haiti. It's also a working language of the United Nations, the European Union and the International Olympic Committee — a genuinely useful language to know well beyond the classroom.

    How the levels work

    Every language deck on Flashlingo comes in three levels, so you can start where you actually are instead of where a textbook assumes you are.

    • Beginner — the 500 highest-frequency words: greetings, numbers, everyday objects and simple phrases.
    • Intermediate — broader everyday vocabulary spread across 19 topic categories, from food and travel to family and work.
    • Expert — less common but genuinely useful words that push you past textbook basics toward natural, fluent vocabulary.

    Tips for learning French vocabulary faster

    • Say new words out loud, not just silently — French pronunciation is a skill in itself.
    • French spelling doesn't always match pronunciation: many final consonants are silent (the “t” in “petit,” for example), so trust your ears as much as your eyes.
    • Notice the gender of nouns (le/la) and try to learn the article together with the word rather than separately.
    • Switch to Hard mode once a category feels easy — typing the word from memory locks it in far better than recognizing it in a list.
    • Ten focused minutes a day beats one long session a week. Spaced repetition is what actually moves words into long-term memory.

    A few words to start with

    Bonjour (hello), Merci (thank you), S'il vous plaît (please), Oui / Non (yes / no), and Au revoir (goodbye) will get you through more real conversations than you'd expect.

    FAQ

    Is French hard for English speakers to learn?

    Relatively approachable — thanks to Norman French's influence on English after 1066, roughly a third of English vocabulary has French or Latin roots, so a lot of words will look more familiar than you expect.

    How long does it take to learn 500 French words?

    It depends on how often you practice, but at around 15 minutes a day most learners work through the Beginner deck in three to four weeks.

    I already know some French — where should I start?

    Start at Beginner anyway. It's a quick way to calibrate the deck to your pace, and you can jump to Intermediate or Expert the moment it feels too easy.
