Mandarin Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

米饭, 面条, 茶 and 买单: the Mandarin words for eating out and talking about food, with characters, pinyin and the measure words you need to order.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

In Mandarin, food words are a great way to see how the language builds vocabulary from a small set of characters. 肉 (ròu) means meat, so 鸡肉 is chicken meat, 猪肉 is pork and 牛肉 is beef. 饭 (fàn) means cooked rice but also a meal, which is why breakfast, lunch and dinner are 早饭, 午饭 and 晚饭. Learn a few of these building blocks and many new words become easy to guess.

Mandarin nouns have no gender and no articles, but you do need a measure word when you count things, and that matters a lot when ordering. The words below are in simplified characters, as used in mainland China and Singapore. Pinyin is given with tone marks; if you are not yet comfortable with the four tones, read our guide to Mandarin tones and pinyin first.

Example sentences

Pinyin in the sentences shows the tone of 一 (yī) as it is actually spoken: it changes to yì before first, second and third tones, and to yí before a fourth tone.

我要一杯茶。
Wǒ yào yì bēi chá.I'd like a cup of tea.
来一碗面条。
Lái yì wǎn miàntiáo.A bowl of noodles, please. (casual ordering)
可以给我菜单吗？
Kěyǐ gěi wǒ càidān ma?Could you give me the menu?
我们点菜吧。

Usage notes

Measure words for food and drink

Between a number and a noun, Mandarin needs a measure word. For food the most useful are 碗 wǎn (bowl): 一碗饭 yì wǎn fàn, a bowl of rice; 杯 bēi (cup, glass): 一杯茶 yì bēi chá, a cup of tea; 瓶 píng (bottle): 两瓶啤酒 liǎng píng píjiǔ, two bottles of beer; and the general 个 ge: 三个包子 sān ge bāozi, three buns. Note that "two" before a measure word is 两 liǎng, not 二 èr.

吃 or 喝?

吃 chī (eat) is used for food and 喝 hē (drink) for liquids, as in English. Soup is "drunk" in Mandarin: 喝汤 hē tāng. In the same way, delicious food is 好吃 hǎochī and a nice-tasting drink is 好喝 hǎohē.

饭 means more than rice

饭 fàn on its own can mean a meal, so 吃饭 chīfàn often just means "to eat" or "to have a meal". When you specifically want a bowl of plain rice, 米饭 mǐfàn is the clearer word.

Talking about what you like

To say what you like to eat, use 喜欢 xǐhuan (to like) followed by the verb: 我喜欢吃饺子 Wǒ xǐhuan chī jiǎozi, I like eating dumplings. To say you are hungry, use 我饿了 Wǒ è le. The particle 了 here shows a change of state: you have become hungry.

Asking for the bill

买单 mǎidān is the everyday way to ask for the bill in mainland China. You will also hear 结账 jiézhàng, which means to settle the bill. Calling 服务员 fúwùyuán is a normal, polite way to get a server's attention.
