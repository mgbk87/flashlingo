Korean Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

밥, 김치, 물 and 계산: the Korean words for ordering and talking about food, in Hangul with Revised Romanization and polite example sentences.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

Food is central to everyday Korean conversation. Friends often ask 밥 먹었어요? (bap meogeosseoyo, "Have you eaten?") as a casual way of checking in, and 밥 means both cooked rice and a meal. Korean meals are usually shared, with rice, soup and several side dishes called 반찬 (banchan) placed in the middle of the table. Rice and soup are usually eaten with a spoon (숟가락), and the side dishes with metal chopsticks (젓가락).

Korean nouns have no gender or articles, and there is usually no need to mark plurals. The main thing to practise is the polite verb ending, especially 주세요 (juseyo, "please give me"), which turns almost any food word into an order. Romanization below follows the official Revised Romanization of Korean, which reflects pronunciation rather than spelling. It is a useful guide, but reading Hangul directly will serve you better in the long run.

Example sentences

Usage notes

잘 먹겠습니다 and 잘 먹었습니다

Before eating, Koreans say 잘 먹겠습니다 (jal meokgetseumnida), literally "I will eat well", as thanks to the host or whoever is paying. After the meal comes 잘 먹었습니다 (jal meogeotseumnida), "I ate well". Note the spaces: 잘 is a separate word from the verb.

Native numbers for ordering

Counting portions uses the native Korean numbers: 하나 hana (one), 둘 dul (two), 셋 set (three). Before a counter they shorten to 한, 두, 세: 한 그릇 han geureut (one bowl), 두 병 du byeong (two bottles), 세 잔 se jan (three glasses or cups). Prices, on the other hand, use the Sino-Korean numbers: 만 원 man won is 10,000 won.

Getting attention: 여기요 and 저기요

In a Korean restaurant it is normal to call staff by saying 여기요 (yeogiyo, "over here") or 저기요 (jeogiyo, "excuse me"). Adding 좀 jom softens a request, like "please" or "a little": 물 좀 주세요.

맛있다 and 맛없다

맛 mat means taste. 맛있다 (masitda) is "tasty, delicious" and 맛없다 (madeopda) is "not tasty". In polite speech: 맛있어요 masisseoyo. It is one of the most useful words you can learn for eating out.

From dictionary form to polite speech

Verbs in the table are in dictionary form, ending in 다. In everyday polite speech the ending changes to 요: 먹다 becomes 먹어요 meogeoyo (I eat), 마시다 becomes 마셔요 masyeoyo (I drink) and 주문하다 becomes 주문해요 jumunhaeyo (I order). To make a simple negative, put 안 an in front: 안 먹어요, I don't eat it.

Quick check

What do you say before starting a meal?잘 먹겠습니다 (jal meokgetseumnida).
How do you order one kimchi stew?김치찌개 하나 주세요 (Gimchijjigae hana juseyo).
What does 밥 mean?Cooked rice, and also a meal in general.
Translate: "Two bottles of beer, please."맥주 두 병 주세요 (Maekju du byeong juseyo).
Which word means delicious: 맛있어요 or 맛없어요?맛있어요 (masisseoyo). 맛없어요 means it doesn't taste good.
