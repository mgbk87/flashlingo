How to Play

Modes, difficulty, scoring, and a simple routine for making words stick.

The basics

Flashlingo is a timed vocabulary quiz. You see an English word and pick or type its translation before the timer runs out. There is no sign-up and nothing to install: choose a language, a level, a difficulty and a game mode, and you are playing.

  1. Pick a language from the home page. There are 12, each with 1,500 words.
  2. Choose a level: Beginner, Intermediate or Expert, 500 words each.
  3. Choose a difficulty: Easy or Hard.
  4. Choose a game mode: By Category, Endless or God Mode.

Easy vs Hard

How you answerPick from four choicesType the answer
Time per word5 seconds10 seconds
PointsStandardDouble
What it trainsRecognising a wordRecalling it from memory

For languages written in other scripts, Hard mode asks you to type the romanization shown on the card: pinyin for Mandarin, romaji for Japanese, and the romanization for Korean and Hindi. In Korean and Hindi, accents, spaces and hyphens are ignored.

In the other languages, including Mandarin pinyin and Japanese romaji, an answer that is right apart from the accents or tone marks counts as a near miss: it is accepted, but earns fewer points. Typing the marks is worth the effort.

The three game modes

Faster answers earn more. You get the full points if you answer while most of the timer is left, and fewer as it runs down. A wrong answer or running out of time costs a life in Endless and God Mode.

High scores

Your best scores are saved in your browser on the device you are playing on, separately for each language and level, with the date and time you set them. There is no account and nothing is sent to us. Clearing your browser data, or switching to a different device or browser, starts you with a clean slate.

A simple weekly routine

The modes work best together. One routine that follows the principles in our article on active recall and spaced repetition:

  1. Day 1: play one new category on Easy, then look over the words you missed in the word list on the page.
  2. Day 2: play the same category on Hard. Typing forces you to recall each word rather than recognise it.
  3. Day 4: play Endless to mix that category with others you have learned.
  4. End of the week: try God Mode. A long run means the words have stuck; an early miss shows you what to review.

Ten minutes a day is plenty. Short, regular sessions beat one long one.
