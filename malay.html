    📖 About Malay

    Why learn Malay with Flashlingo?

    Malay is the national language of Malaysia and Brunei and one of Singapore's official languages. It's closely related to Indonesian, and together the Malay–Indonesian language family is spoken by close to 290 million people across Southeast Asia.

    How the levels work

    Every language deck on Flashlingo comes in three levels, so you can start where you actually are instead of where a textbook assumes you are.

    • Beginner — the 500 highest-frequency words: greetings, numbers, everyday objects and simple phrases.
    • Intermediate — broader everyday vocabulary spread across 24 topic categories, from food and travel to family and work.
    • Expert — less common but genuinely useful words that push you past textbook basics toward natural, fluent vocabulary.

    Tips for learning Malay vocabulary faster

    • Malay uses the Latin alphabet, so unlike Mandarin or Japanese there's no new script to learn — you can start reading it immediately.
    • Verbs don't conjugate for tense, person or number, which makes vocabulary genuinely the hardest part of Malay — once you know the words, building sentences is comparatively simple.
    • Plurals are often formed by repeating the word (buku = book, buku-buku = books) — a pattern worth recognizing early since it looks unusual coming from English.
    • Many everyday words are borrowed from Arabic, Sanskrit, Chinese dialects and English, a reflection of the region's long trading history — you'll spot familiar-looking words more often than you'd expect.
    • Because grammar is comparatively light, time spent here goes almost entirely toward vocabulary — exactly what a flashcard deck is built for.

    A few words to start with

    Selamat pagi (good morning) or Halo (hello), Terima kasih (thank you), Tolong (please), Ya / Tidak (yes / no), and Selamat tinggal (goodbye) will get you through most everyday exchanges.

    FAQ

    Is Malay similar to Indonesian?

    Very — the two are mutually intelligible for most everyday vocabulary, though spelling conventions and some words differ between them.

    Is Malay easy to learn?

    The lack of verb conjugation, plural rules and grammatical gender makes the grammar simpler than in many languages — vocabulary is where the real work is, which is exactly what this deck focuses on.

    Where should I start if I'm completely new?

    Beginner — it covers the 500 highest-frequency words, so what you learn first is what you'll actually reach for in a real conversation.
