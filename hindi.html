    📖 About Hindi

    Why learn Hindi with Flashlingo?

    Hindi is one of the most widely spoken languages in the world, with around 600 million speakers when people who use it as a second language are included. It is an official language of India's central government and the everyday language of Bollywood films, songs and much of North Indian life, and the spoken language is closely related to Urdu, which widens how far your vocabulary will carry.

    How the Hindi levels work

    The Hindi deck has three levels of 500 words each, so you can start where you actually are.

    • Beginner — 500 words across 24 topics: everyday essentials such as food, home, transport, numbers and greetings. For example: सेब (seb, apple), गाड़ी (gāṛī, car), फ़ोन (fon, phone).
    • Intermediate — 500 words across 19 topics: vocabulary for work, travel, technology, health, money and the news. For example: घर से काम (ghar se kām, remote work), पासपोर्ट (pāsporṭ, passport), ऐप (aip, app).
    • Expert — 500 words across 17 topics: formal and academic vocabulary for law, science, philosophy, economics and the arts. For example: अहिंसा (ahinsā, nonviolence), योग (yog, yoga), कृष्ण विवर (kŕṣṇa vivar, black hole).

    Hindi vocabulary draws on three main sources: inherited and Sanskrit-derived words such as विद्यालय (vidyālay, school), words from Persian and Arabic such as क़ानून (qānūn, law) and बाज़ार (bāzār, market), and English loanwords such as फ़ोन (fon, phone) and ऐप (aip, app). Formal and technical terms are usually built from Sanskrit roots.

    Tips for learning Hindi vocabulary faster

    • Hindi is written in Devanagari, where each consonant carries a built-in "a" sound and vowel signs attach to it. Every word in this deck is shown with its romanization, so you can learn the sounds first and pick up the script as you go.
    • The romanization follows the scheme used by Wiktionary: a line over a vowel (ā, ī, ū) makes it long, a dot under t, d or n (ṭ, ḍ, ṇ) marks a retroflex sound, ś and ṣ are "sh" sounds, a tilde (ã) marks nasalisation, and x, ġ, q and z mark sounds borrowed from Persian, written in Devanagari with a dot (nukta).
    • Learn word-building endings. -विज्ञान (-vijñān, study of) appears in भूविज्ञान (bhūvijñān, geology), जीवविज्ञान (jīvvijñān, biology) and मनोविज्ञान (manovijñān, psychology).
    • Verbs are listed in the infinitive, which ends in -ना (-nā), such as खाना (khānā, to eat). Many verbs are made by adding करना (karnā, to do) to a noun, as in मदद करना (madad karnā, to help).
    • In Hard mode, accents are optional and common spellings are accepted: you can type sh for ś, kh for x, and double vowels such as aa or ee for long ones.

    A few words to start with

    नमस्ते (namaste, hello / hi), धन्यवाद (dhanyavād, thank you), हाँ (hā̃, yes), नहीं (nahī̃, no) and अलविदा (alvidā, goodbye) are a good everyday starting set.

    FAQ

    Do I need to read Devanagari to use this deck?

    No — every word is shown with its romanization, so you can build spoken vocabulary first. Seeing the Devanagari next to it on every card is a painless way to start recognising the letters.

    How do I answer in Hard mode?

    Type the romanization shown on the card. Accents, capital letters, spaces and hyphens are ignored, and common spellings such as sh, kh and aa are accepted.

    Is Hindi grammar hard?

    The verb usually comes at the end of the sentence, and every noun is masculine or feminine, which affects adjectives and verbs. On the other hand, spelling is very regular: once you know Devanagari, you can read almost any word aloud.
