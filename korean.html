    📖 About Korean

    Why learn Korean with Flashlingo?

    Korean has around 80 million speakers, mainly in South Korea and North Korea, with large communities in China, the United States and Japan. Its reach has grown far beyond the peninsula thanks to Korean film, television, music and food, which gives learners a steady stream of real-world listening practice.

    How the Korean levels work

    The Korean deck has three levels of 500 words each, so you can start where you actually are.

    • Beginner — 500 words across 24 topics: everyday essentials such as food, home, transport, numbers and greetings. For example: 사과 (sagwa, apple), 자동차 (jadongcha, car), 전화 (jeonhwa, phone).
    • Intermediate — 500 words across 19 topics: vocabulary for work, travel, technology, health, money and the news. For example: 재택근무 (jaetaekgeunmu, remote work), 여권 (yeogwon, passport), 앱 (aep, app).
    • Expert — 500 words across 17 topics: formal and academic vocabulary for law, science, philosophy, economics and the arts. For example: 판례법 (pallyebeop, case law), 자유 의지 (jayuuiji, free will), 블랙홀 (beullaekhol, black hole).

    Notice the two big sources of modern vocabulary: English loanwords written in Hangul, such as 앱 (aep, app) and 블랙홀 (beullaekhol, black hole), and Sino-Korean words built from Chinese-derived roots, such as 판례법 (pallyebeop, case law) — the same building blocks behind most formal and academic terms.

    Tips for learning Korean vocabulary faster

    • Korean is written in Hangul, an alphabet of 24 basic letters grouped into syllable blocks. Every word in this deck is paired with its romanization so you can start on pronunciation straight away, but Hangul is quick to learn and is well worth picking up early.
    • Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system, which reflects how a word is pronounced rather than letter-by-letter spelling — for example 정류장 (jeongnyujang, bus stop) is written with ㄹ but sounds closer to jeongnyujang.
    • Learn Sino-Korean roots as you go. The ending 학 (hak, study of) appears in 생물학 (saengmulhak, biology), 화학 (hwahak, chemistry) and 언어학 (eoneohak, linguistics), so one root unlocks dozens of words.
    • Verbs and adjectives are listed in their dictionary form ending in 다 (da), such as 먹다 (meokda, to eat). In conversation they are conjugated for politeness and tense.
    • Korean has two sets of numbers. This deck uses native Korean numbers like 하나 (hana, one) for one to ninety-nine, plus Sino-Korean 백 (baek, hundred) and 천 (cheon, thousand).

    A few words to start with

    안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo, hello), 감사합니다 (gamsahamnida, thank you), 네 (ne, yes), 아니요 (aniyo, no) and 안녕히 가세요 (annyeonghi gaseyo, goodbye) are a good everyday starting set.

    FAQ

    Do I need to read Hangul to use this deck?

    No — every word is shown with its romanization, so you can build spoken vocabulary first. Seeing the Hangul next to it on every card is also a painless way to start recognising the letters.

    How do I answer in Hard mode?

    Type the romanization shown on the card. Capital letters, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes are ignored, so han'guk and hanguk both count.

    Is Korean grammar hard?

    The verb comes at the end of the sentence and small particles mark the subject and object, which takes some adjustment. Verbs don't change for "I, you, he", though — the main thing to learn is the level of politeness.
