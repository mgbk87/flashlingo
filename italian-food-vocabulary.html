Italian Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

Pane, formaggio, caffè and il conto: the Italian words for eating out and shopping for food, with articles and the plurals that catch learners out.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

Many Italian food words have travelled into English, but they do not always keep their meaning. Order a latte in Italy and you will most likely be given a glass of milk, because latte simply means milk. Ask for un caffè and you get an espresso. Learning the everyday words properly helps you avoid these small surprises.

Italian nouns are masculine or feminine, and the article changes with the first sound of the word: il before most masculine nouns, lo before s + consonant, z and a few others, la for feminine nouns, and l' before a vowel. Italian is read almost exactly as it is written; the hints show the stressed syllable in capitals and a doubled letter where the consonant is held longer.

Usage notes

Plurals change the ending

Italian plurals usually change the final vowel: -o becomes -i (il pollo → i polli), -a becomes -e (la mela → le mele), and -e becomes -i (il pesce → i pesci). The article changes too: il → i, lo/l' (m.) → gli, la/l' (f.) → le.

The odd one: l'uovo, le uova

A handful of masculine nouns become feminine in the plural with an -a ending. The one you will need most at the market is l'uovo (egg), plural le uova: sei uova, six eggs.

Primo, secondo, dolce

A traditional Italian menu is divided into antipasti (starters), primi (pasta, risotto or soup), secondi (meat or fish), contorni (side dishes) and dolci (desserts). You do not have to order every course; many people choose just a primo or a secondo. Before eating, Italians wish each other Buon appetito!

Words that don't mean what you expect

Latte is milk; if you want the coffee drink, ask for un caffellatte or un cappuccino. Peperoni means peppers (the vegetable), not a spicy sausage. A caffè on its own is a small espresso.

Coffee at the counter

In many Italian bars, people drink their coffee standing at the counter (al banco), and sitting at a table can cost more. In busy bars you sometimes pay first at the till (la cassa) and then show your receipt to the barista. Ordering with Vorrei… (I would like) is always polite.
