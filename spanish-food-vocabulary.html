Spanish Food Vocabulary: 30 Words with Example Sentences

From pan and queso to la cuenta, these are the Spanish food words you will actually use, with notes on where Spain and Latin America say things differently.

By Melvin, founder of Flashlingo · Published 9 November 2026

Spanish is spoken in more than twenty countries, and food is where regional differences show up most. A potato is la patata in Madrid but la papa in Mexico City or Buenos Aires; juice is el zumo in Spain and el jugo across most of Latin America. None of these is more correct than the other, so in the tables we flag the pairs and let you choose the variety you are learning.

Every noun is given with its definite article: el for masculine, la for feminine. Spanish spelling is very regular, so the sound hints mainly show where the stress falls (capital letters). One thing to know: in most of Spain, z and c before e/i sound like English "th", while in Latin America and parts of southern Spain they sound like "s". The hints below use the "s" sound.

Usage notes

Spain vs Latin America

Besides patata/papa and zumo/jugo, watch out for the word for waiter: camarero in Spain, mesero in Mexico and several other countries, and mozo in Argentina. Customers in Spain often order with ¿Me pone…? ("Could you give me…?"), which is less common in Latin America, where ¿Me da…? or Quisiera… are typical.

Why el agua is feminine

Agua is a feminine noun, but it takes el in the singular because it starts with a stressed a sound: el agua. Adjectives stay feminine (el agua fría), and the plural goes back to las aguas.

Comida, almuerzo and comer

La comida means "food" in general, but in Spain it is also the usual name for the main midday meal, and comer can mean "to have lunch". In much of Latin America that meal is el almuerzo, while in Spain el almuerzo can also mean a mid-morning snack. Context will usually make it clear.

Being hungry

Spanish uses tener (to have) for hunger and thirst: Tengo hambre (I'm hungry), Tengo sed (I'm thirsty). Saying estoy hambre is a common learner error.
