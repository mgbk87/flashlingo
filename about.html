About Flashlingo

Fun, free and just a little bit addictive vocabulary practice, built in Singapore.

Who builds Flashlingo

Flashlingo is built and run by Melvin, from Singapore. My day job is in business development, and it has me working with teams across Asia Pacific, in places such as China, India, Vietnam and Malaysia. Working across that many languages is what got me interested in a faster way to build vocabulary in short, spare moments.

Flashlingo is built for that situation: a few spare minutes, a phone, and a vocabulary list that actually sticks. It started as a simple French quiz and has grown to 12 languages, adding the languages I meet at work and the ones players ask for.

Where we're based

Singapore is a city where switching between English, Mandarin, Malay and Tamil is part of everyday life. That multilingual setting is a big part of why Flashlingo exists, and why Asian languages such as Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Malay, Vietnamese, Indonesian and Hindi sit alongside French, Spanish, German, Italian and Portuguese.

What Flashlingo does

Flashlingo turns vocabulary building into a game. Pick a language, choose your level, and race the clock through short quizzes. There are no sign-up walls and no subscriptions: just a timer and 1,500 words per language, split into Beginner, Intermediate and Expert levels of 500 words each.

New to the site? How to play explains the modes, difficulty levels and scoring.

How we make the word lists

Accuracy matters more to us than size. Our newer decks are checked word by word against Wiktionary dictionary data published by Kaikki.org, and we are reviewing the older decks the same way. When a concept has no good translation, we replace it rather than invent one. The full process is described in how we build and check our word lists, and every language page lists all 1,500 words so you can see exactly what is in the deck.

Our approach to learning

  • Recall, not re-reading. Every question makes you retrieve a word from memory, which is what makes it stick. The research behind this is in our article on active recall and spaced repetition.
  • Short and often. Rounds take a few minutes, so daily practice fits around real life.
  • Grow at your own pace. Three levels and two difficulties mean there is always a next step, without rushing.

Why it's free

Building vocabulary should be open to anyone. Flashlingo is supported by advertising, which keeps every language, level and game mode free. High scores are stored only in your own browser; our privacy policy explains how advertising cookies work.
